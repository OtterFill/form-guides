# W-8BEN: fields and common mistakes

Form W-8BEN is the form an individual who is not a U.S. person gives to a U.S. payer to certify their foreign status and, if they qualify, claim a reduced tax-treaty rate. You give it to whoever pays you, never to the IRS.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The U.S. payer, platform or broker that asked for it (the withholding agent), not the IRS |
| How long it is valid | Until the end of the third calendar year after the year you sign it, unless your situation changes sooner |
| Cost | Free |
| Without it | The payer may withhold 30% of U.S.-source payments |

## Who fills it in

You fill it in if all of these are true:

- You are an individual, not a company.
- You are not a U.S. citizen or a U.S. resident for tax purposes.
- You are the person who actually owns the income (the beneficial owner).
- A U.S. payer, platform or broker has asked you for it.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong form.** A company needs W-8BEN-E and a U.S. citizen or resident needs W-9. A W-8BEN from the wrong person is invalid.
- **Sending it to the IRS.** You hand the form to the payer, platform or broker that asked for it. The IRS does not collect it.
- **A P.O. box or an agent's address on line 3.** Line 3 must be your real home address in your country of residence.
- **Mixing up lines 5, 6a and 8.** Line 5 is a U.S. number, line 6a is your home-country tax number and line 8 is your date of birth. Putting your national ID on line 5 is a common slip.
- **Writing the date day first.** The form wants month first. 03-07-1990 means 7 March, not 3 July.
- **A typed name instead of a signature.** Sign it, or use the payer's online signing system. A typed name alone does not count.
- **A treaty country that does not match your address.** Line 9 is your country of tax residence. Claiming a treaty of another country can get the claim rejected.
- **Letting it go out of date.** Send a new form within 30 days after a change, such as a move to another country or becoming a U.S. resident, and again once your form expires.
