# SS-5: fields and common mistakes

Form SS-5, “Application for a Social Security Card”, is the form for asking the U.S. Social Security Administration for a first Social Security number, a replacement card, or a change to your record such as a new name. U.S. citizens and noncitizens with permission to work from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) fill it in and bring it with original documents to a Social Security office.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The Social Security Administration (SSA), at a local Social Security office or Social Security Card Center |
| Who fills it in | You, or a parent or legal guardian for a child; the form is the same for citizens and noncitizens |
| Documents | Originals or copies certified by the office that issued them; no photocopies or notarized copies. They are returned to you |
| In person | Required if you are 12 or older and have never had a Social Security number |
| Cost | Free. This guide follows the edition marked SS-5 (12-2024) |

## Who fills it in

You use Form SS-5 if:

- You are a U.S. citizen who never received a Social Security number, for example a child whose number was not requested at the hospital.
- You are a noncitizen who is in the United States lawfully with permission to work from DHS, for example a permanent resident, a worker on a work visa, someone with an Employment Authorization Document, or a student with authorized work, and you did not request a number through your immigration application.
- Your card was lost, stolen or damaged and you need a replacement, and you cannot request it online.
- Your name changed, you became a U.S. citizen, or your record shows wrong information that you want corrected.

## Common mistakes

- **Bringing photocopies or notarized copies.** SSA accepts only originals or copies certified by the office that issued them, and returns them to you.
- **Bringing a receipt instead of the document.** A receipt showing that you applied for an immigration document is not accepted. Wait until you have the document itself.
- **Applying the day you arrive.** SSA suggests waiting 10 days after you arrive in the United States, and students 48 hours after reporting to their school, so that DHS records can be checked.
- **Applying as a student without a job.** SSA issues a number only with work authorization. For on-campus work, bring a letter from your school official and from your employer; SSA cannot process it if the work starts more than 30 days after you apply.
- **Checking “Legal Alien Not Allowed To Work” without a letter.** This box needs a letter from a government agency that requires the number. If you may work, check “Legal Alien Allowed To Work”.
- **A name that does not match your documents.** Write the name as on your identity or immigration document. Spelling differences slow the check.
- **Abbreviating the place of birth or the address.** Items 3 and 16 say “Do Not Abbreviate”: write city, state or country, and street names in full.
- **Skipping items 12 and 13.** If you ever had a card or applied for one, check Yes in item 11 and fill in items 12 and 13.
- **Mailing an application you must make in person.** If you are 12 or older and never had a number, you must go to an office.
- **Using a pencil or another ink color.** Write legibly with black or blue ink only, and sign by hand.
