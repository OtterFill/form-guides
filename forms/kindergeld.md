# KG 1: fields and common mistakes

The Antrag auf Kindergeld (form KG 1) is the paper application for Germany’s child benefit, sent with one Anlage Kind for each child to the Familienkasse of the Federal Employment Agency. You fill it in if you live in Germany (or are fully taxable there) and a child under 18, or an older child in training, lives with you; only one person receives Kindergeld for each child.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Where it goes | The Familienkasse responsible for you, usually the one for where you live; or apply online at familienkasse.de |
| What you send | KG 1 once, plus one Anlage Kind for each child; Anlage EU, Anlage Ausland or a copy of your residence permit where the form asks for it |
| How | Signed on paper by post or fax, or online; not by email or phone |
| Back payment | At most 6 months before the month in which the Familienkasse receives your application (§ 70 Income Tax Act, EStG) |
| Amount | The same for every child since 2023; it is set by law and changes, so check the Familienkasse’s page |

## Who fills it in

You fill in KG 1 if:

- You live in Germany, or you live abroad but are fully taxable in Germany, and you have your own, adopted, step-, grand- or foster child in your household.
- The child is under 18; or 18 to 24 and in school, training or studies, in a voluntary service, between two stages of training (at most 4 months), or without a training place; or under 21 and registered as a jobseeker; or has a disability that began before the age of 25.
- You are a citizen of Germany, another EU or EEA country or Switzerland; or a citizen of another country with a residence permit that entitles you to Kindergeld, for example a settlement permit.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving a tax ID empty.** Your tax ID and the child’s are required whenever they have been issued; without them the Familienkasse cannot pay. Find them in the BZSt letter or your tax papers, or request them again on bzst.de, which sends them only by post.
- **Applying months after the birth or the move.** Kindergeld is paid back for at most 6 months before the month the Familienkasse receives your application. Apply as soon as you can, even if a document will follow.
- **Forgetting an Anlage Kind.** Each child you apply for needs their own Anlage Kind, and the number of them goes at the top of KG 1. On paper the main form alone is not a complete application.
- **Both parents applying.** Only one person receives Kindergeld for a child. When you live together, decide who applies; the other signs the consent at the end of KG 1.
- **Missing the extra papers for your nationality.** Citizens of another EU or EEA country add the Anlage EU; citizens of countries outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland enclose a copy of their residence permit. If a parent or child lives or works abroad, the Anlage Kind questions 6 and 7 and the Anlage Ausland matter.
- **Sending the form by email.** The Familienkasse does not accept applications by email or phone. Send the signed paper form by post or fax, or apply online.
- **Applying for an adult child without proof.** For a child aged 18 or over, enclose proof of school, studies, training or voluntary service, or the form KG 11a for a child without a training place or registered as a jobseeker.
- **Not reporting changes afterwards.** You must tell the Familienkasse at once about changes such as a move, a child leaving your household or finishing training, or work abroad. Kindergeld paid wrongly must be repaid, even if you were not at fault.
