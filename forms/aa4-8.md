# AA4/8: fields and common mistakes

The AA4/8 is the Agenzia delle Entrate’s form to apply for an Italian tax code (codice fiscale), to report a change in your details, or to ask for a codice fiscale card or a replacement health card (Tessera Sanitaria). You fill it in if you need a codice fiscale and no other office assigns it to you, for example as an EU citizen moving to Italy, a non-resident student or worker, or someone abroad who has to sign a contract or accept an inheritance in Italy.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Where it goes | Any Agenzia delle Entrate office, by appointment, or by certified email (PEC) to an office; if you live abroad, also the Italian consulate in your country of residence |
| What to bring | The form signed, and a valid identity document, such as a passport (with a visa if you need one), a residence permit or an ID card valid for travel; for an application for someone else, your own ID and a statement of the reason |
| Cost | The form and its instructions are free to download and print; the agency’s pages for this request name no fee |
| What you receive | A certificate of attribution of the codice fiscale, then by post a codice fiscale card (if you are not in the national health service) or a Tessera Sanitaria (if you are) |
| How long it lasts | The codice fiscale does not expire; it is a 16-character code built from your name, sex, date and place of birth |

## Who fills it in

You fill in the AA4/8 if:

- You are an EU citizen and need a codice fiscale to live, study or work in Italy.
- You live legally in Italy but were never given a codice fiscale, or you are a non-resident student, worker or visitor who needs one (for example a student staying less than 90 days).
- You live abroad and need a codice fiscale for something in Italy, such as a contract, a lease or an inheritance; you then apply at the Italian consulate or through someone you authorise in Italy.
- You already have a codice fiscale and need to change your details, get a certificate of it, or replace a lost, stolen, damaged or undelivered card.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a code from an online calculator.** A code worked out by a website has no value, even if it looks right. Only the code the Agenzia delle Entrate issues is valid; get it with this form or from the office that assigns it to you.
- **Applying when another office assigns it.** If you are a non-EU citizen applying for a residence permit, the Questura or the Sportello Unico per l’Immigrazione assigns your code. You do not need to go to the agency for it.
- **Writing your birth town instead of the country.** If you were born outside Italy, write the country in Quadro B and EE as the province. Your town abroad does not go on this form.
- **Names that do not match your ID.** Write your names in full, as in your passport or ID card, without titles, and replace letters such as ü with plain letters (MUELLER). A difference can give you a code that does not match your documents.
- **Leaving Quadro D blank when you live abroad.** Non-residents must fill in every field of Quadro D. Your card is posted to the address the agency has for you, so check it.
- **Ticking RICHIESTA TESSERINO when you are in the health service.** If you are entitled to the SSN, you get a Tessera Sanitaria automatically; the codice fiscale card is only for people who are not.
- **Forgetting to sign.** The form is void without the applicant’s signature. If someone hands it in for you, you also sign the Delega, and every field of it must be filled in.
- **Asking for a replacement health card that is about to expire.** A new Tessera Sanitaria is sent automatically before it expires. Use box 5 only for a card that was stolen, lost, never delivered or damaged.
