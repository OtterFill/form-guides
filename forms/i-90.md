# I-90: fields and common mistakes

Form I-90, “Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card”, is the form you file with USCIS to renew a green card that has expired or will expire within six months, or to replace one that was lost, stolen, damaged, never received or has wrong information. You fill it in yourself if you are a permanent resident; a parent or legal guardian may sign for a child under 14.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | USCIS, online through a USCIS online account or on paper by mail to the address on the official page |
| Who fills it in | You, the permanent resident; a parent or legal guardian may sign for a child under 14 |
| What you get | A new Permanent Resident Card (green card), mailed to a U.S. address if USCIS approves |
| Key answer | Part 2: one reason for applying, from Section A for permanent residents or Section B for conditional residents |
| Cost | Depends on your reason; some reasons are free and filing online costs less than on paper: check the fee schedule (Form G-1055) linked from the official page. This guide follows the edition dated 01/20/25 |

## Who fills it in

You file Form I-90 if you are a permanent resident and:

- Your green card has expired or will expire within the next six months.
- Your card was lost, stolen, destroyed or damaged, or USCIS issued it but you never received it.
- Your card has wrong information, or your name or other details legally changed after it was issued.
- You turned 14 and your card was issued before then; you start or stop living as a commuter from Canada or Mexico; your status was automatically converted to permanent residence; or you hold an old card such as Form I-151.

## Common mistakes

- **Using Form I-90 for an expiring 2-year card.** A conditional resident whose card expires within 90 days cannot use Form I-90. File Form I-751 (marriage) or Form I-829 (investment) to remove the conditions.
- **Renewing too early.** Reason 2.f is for a card that has expired or will expire within six months. If yours expires later, USCIS may deny the application.
- **Choosing “never received” for a card that was delivered.** Use 2.b only if the card came back to USCIS as undeliverable. If it was mailed to the address you gave, file as lost (2.a).
- **Blaming DHS for your own error.** Reason 2.d is only for mistakes DHS made, and you must send the original card. If the details were wrong when you gave them, or changed later, use 2.e.
- **Writing the date you entered as the Date of Admission.** Item 15 is the “Resident Since” date on your card. If you adjusted status, it is the approval date, not your arrival date.
- **Using the metric system.** Write your height in feet and inches and your weight in pounds. For example, 5 feet 9 inches is “5” and “09”.
- **Leaving out the key fields.** USCIS rejects a form without your family name, mailing address and date of birth, and any unsigned form. Sign Part 5 by hand.
- **Using the wrong edition or leaving out pages.** On paper, USCIS may reject a form with missing pages or pages from different editions. Download it from the official page and send all 7 pages.
- **Giving a foreign mailing address.** USCIS does not mail green cards abroad. If you plan to travel and your card is about to expire, USCIS recommends getting the new card before you leave.
- **Picking the wrong reason at 14.** Reasons 2.g.1 and 2.g.2 apply only within 30 days after your 14th birthday. Later, the form says to use 2.j; if your card has expired, use 2.f.
