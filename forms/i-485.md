# I-485: fields and common mistakes

Form I-485, “Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status”, is the form you file with USCIS to get a green card without leaving the United States. You fill it in yourself if you are in the United States and qualify in an immigrant category, usually through a family member, an employer, asylum or refugee status.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | USCIS: by mail to the address for your category on the Direct Filing Addresses page, or online for some categories; in removal proceedings, the immigration court |
| Who fills it in | You, in the United States; every family member applying, including each child, files a separate Form I-485 |
| When | In general, only once an immigrant visa is immediately available to you; some categories may file together with the petition |
| Length | 24 pages in 14 parts, with 41 pages of instructions. Since September 18, 2026 USCIS accepts only the edition dated 09/18/26, which this guide follows |
| Cost | Changes over time: check the fee schedule (Form G-1055) linked from the official page. Forms I-765 and I-131 filed with it have their own fees |

## Who fills it in

You file Form I-485 if you are physically present in the United States and one of these applies:

- You are an immediate relative of a U.S. citizen: a spouse, an unmarried child under 21, or a parent of a citizen who is at least 21, including K-1 fiancé(e)s who married within 90 days.
- You are in a family or employment preference category with a petition filed or approved, and your priority date is current in the Visa Bulletin.
- You were granted asylum, or admitted as a refugee, and have been physically present in the United States for 1 year since.
- You qualify as a special immigrant, a VAWA self-petitioner, a T or U nonimmigrant, a Diversity Visa selectee, or under a program such as the Cuban Adjustment Act.
- You are the spouse or child of a principal applicant and file as a derivative applicant in a category that allows it.

## Common mistakes

- **Using an old edition.** Since September 18, 2026 USCIS rejects the 01/20/25 and 09/04/26 editions. Download the form from the official page on the day you print, and send all pages from the same edition.
- **Filing before a visa is available.** File only when your priority date is current in the Visa Bulletin chart USCIS says to use that month, or when your category lets you file together with the petition.
- **Leaving answers blank.** Write “N/A” when a question does not apply and “None” when the answer to a number question is zero. Answer every question in Part 9.
- **Sending it without Form I-693.** If you need the medical exam, send the I-693 with the I-485 or USCIS may reject it. By mail, keep it in the civil surgeon’s sealed envelope; online, upload it and keep the original.
- **Hiding an old or sealed arrest.** Part 9 asks about arrests and charges anywhere and at any age, even if expunged. Answer “Yes”, explain in Part 14 and send certified court records.
- **Gaps in addresses or jobs.** Cover 5 years of addresses and of jobs or schools, with “unemployed” and your means of support where you had no job. Make them match your earlier visa applications.
- **Ticking the wrong box in Part 3.** Most family-based applicants tick 1.f and send Form I-864. Ticking an exemption you do not qualify for, or leaving out a required I-864, leads to denial.
- **Traveling without advance parole.** If you leave the United States while the I-485 is pending, USCIS treats it as abandoned unless you have advance parole (Form I-131) or a valid H-1B, H-4, L, V, K-3 or K-4 status and visa.
- **Signing with a typed name, or not at all.** Sign Part 10 by hand. USCIS rejects an unsigned form; a photocopy or scan of a hand-signed form is accepted.
- **Sending documents without translations.** Every document in another language needs a full English translation and a certification with the translator’s signature, printed name, date and contact details.
