# W-4: fields and common mistakes

Form W-4 is the form you give your employer so it withholds the right amount of federal income tax from each paycheck. Every employee in the U.S. fills it in when starting a job, and gives it to the employer, not to the IRS.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | Your employer (payroll or HR), not the IRS |
| Who fills it in | Employees paid wages; self-employed people and nonresident aliens follow other rules |
| How long it is valid | Until you give a new one; a claim of exemption lasts for one year only |
| Cost | Free |
| Without a form | Your employer treats you as a single person with no other entries, which can mean too much tax withheld |

## Who fills it in

You fill it in if:

- You are starting a job where you receive wages as an employee.
- Your situation changes and you want your employer to withhold a different amount, for example after marriage, a new child, a second job or a change in income.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking for allowances.** Old guides tell you to claim a number of allowances. The current form has none; your answers are the filing status and the steps.
- **Skipping Step 2 with a second job.** Without it, each employer withholds as if that job were your only one, and you can owe tax in April. Use the estimator, the worksheet or the two-job box.
- **Claiming dependents on both forms.** Complete Steps 3 to 4(b) on only one form: the one for the highest paying job. Doing it twice, or for both spouses, means too little is withheld.
- **A name that does not match the Social Security card.** Write the name exactly as on the card. A mismatch can mean your earnings are not credited to you.
- **Checking head of household when you do not qualify.** It applies only if you are unmarried and pay more than half the costs of a home for yourself and a qualifying person.
- **Claiming exemption without meeting both conditions.** You need no federal income tax liability last year and none expected this year. Otherwise you may owe tax and penalties when you file.
- **Forgetting that exemption expires.** An exempt claim is for one year. Give your employer a new form by February 16, 2027, or withholding starts again.
- **Sending the form to the IRS.** The form says to give it to your employer, not to the IRS.
- **Never updating it.** After a marriage, a new child, a second job or a large refund or tax bill, complete a new form or use the estimator.
