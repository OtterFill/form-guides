# I-9: fields and common mistakes

Form I-9, “Employment Eligibility Verification”, is the form every new U.S. employee completes with their employer to show who they are and that they may work in the United States. You fill in Section 1 yourself, then show your employer original documents, and the employer completes Section 2.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | Your employer, who keeps it; it is not sent to USCIS or ICE |
| Who fills it in | You, for Section 1; your employer, for Section 2 and Supplement B |
| When | Section 1 by your first day of employment, but not before you accept a job offer; documents within 3 business days after your first day |
| Documents | One document from List A, or one from List B plus one from List C; originals, not expired |
| Cost | Free; nothing is filed with USCIS. This guide follows the edition dated 01/20/25 |

## Who fills it in

You fill in Section 1 if:

- You are starting work for an employer in the United States and receive wages or other pay, whether you are a U.S. citizen or not.
- You are rehired by an employer more than 3 years after your first Form I-9 was completed: the employer then needs a new form.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing “lawful permanent resident” as a refugee or asylee.** Refugees and asylees check box 4, “An alien authorized to work”, not box 3.
- **Leaving out the number under box 4.** If you check box 4 you must also write an A-Number, an I-94 number, or a foreign passport number with its country.
- **Leaving the work expiration date blank or guessing it.** Copy the date from your work authorization document. Write N/A only if the authorization truly has no end date.
- **Entering an ITIN as a Social Security number.** An ITIN is not a Social Security number. If you have no Social Security number yet, leave the box blank.
- **Back-dating the signature.** Write the day you actually sign. Back-dating is not allowed.
- **Dropping part of your name.** Keep hyphens and apostrophes. If you have one name only, write it as the last name and “Unknown” as the first name.
- **Bringing copies or expired documents.** Bring originals that have not expired. Only a certified copy of a birth certificate may be a copy.
- **Offering the wrong mix of documents.** Show one List A document, or one List B and one List C. A List B document alone is not enough.
- **Checking a box that is not true.** You sign under penalty of perjury. Check only the box that matches your real status.
- **Letting a helper sign for you.** A preparer or translator completes Supplement A, but you still sign Section 1 yourself.
