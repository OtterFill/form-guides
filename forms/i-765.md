# I-765: fields and common mistakes

Form I-765, “Application for Employment Authorization”, is the form you file with USCIS to ask for permission to work in the United States or for a work permit card (EAD). You fill it in yourself, while you are in the United States, under the one eligibility category that fits your situation.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | USCIS, online through a USCIS account or on paper by mail; the mailing address depends on your reason for applying and your category |
| Who fills it in | You, the applicant, while you are in the United States; a parent or legal guardian may sign for a child under 14 |
| What you get | An Employment Authorization Document (Form I-766), mailed to your U.S. mailing address if USCIS approves |
| Key answer | Item 27, your eligibility category: one code such as (c)(8), (c)(9) or (c)(3)(B), from the instructions |
| Cost | Depends on your category and changes over time: check the fee schedule linked from the official page. This guide follows the edition dated 08/21/25 |

## Who fills it in

You file Form I-765 if you are in the United States and:

- You fit one of the eligibility categories in the instructions, for example as an asylum applicant, an adjustment of status applicant with a pending Form I-485, an F-1 student asking for Optional Practical Training (OPT), a parolee or a TPS applicant.
- Your status already lets you work, as for refugees and asylees, and you want an EAD as proof.
- You need to renew your EAD, or replace one that was lost, stolen or damaged, or that has a mistake that was not USCIS’s fault.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing the wrong eligibility category.** Copy the code from the instructions for your exact situation. A pending benefit often has a different code from a granted one: pending asylum is (c)(8), granted asylum is (a)(5).
- **Entering more than one category.** Write only one code in Item 27: the instructions say to enter only one category number.
- **Using the wrong edition or leaving out pages.** On paper, USCIS may reject a form with missing pages or pages from different editions. Download the form from the official page and send all 7 pages, even blank ones.
- **Signing with a typed or stamped name, or not at all.** Sign Part 3 by hand. USCIS rejects an unsigned form; a photocopy or scan of a hand-signed form is accepted.
- **Leaving items blank.** Write “N/A” when a question does not apply and “None” when the answer to a number question is zero, unless the form says otherwise.
- **Using an old I-94 number.** Enter the I-94 of your most recent entry. Look it up again on the CBP website if you have traveled since.
- **Filing OPT outside the window.** For post-completion OPT, file up to 90 days before and no later than 60 days after your program end date, and within 30 days of your DSO’s recommendation. USCIS denies late requests.
- **Filing a (c)(8) application too early.** You must wait 150 days after filing your asylum application, not counting delays you caused. USCIS may reject a form filed before then.
- **Giving the company’s tax number for STEM OPT.** Item 28.c asks for the E-Verify Company ID, not the employer’s EIN. Ask your employer for it.
- **Photos that do not meet the rules.** On paper, send 2 identical recent color passport-style photos, unretouched, with your name and A-Number lightly written on the back. Retouched photos delay the case.
