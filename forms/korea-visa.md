# Korea visa: fields and common mistakes

The Visa Application Form (사증발급신청서) is the form you complete to apply for a South Korean visa at a Korean embassy or consulate. Every foreign national who needs a visa fills in their own form, in Korean or English, and signs it; a parent or legal guardian signs for a child under 17.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The Korean embassy or consulate for your country or area of residence; a visa agency may lodge it for you |
| Who fills it in | Every foreign national who needs a Korean visa, each with their own form; a parent or legal guardian signs for a child under 17 |
| The form | 5 pages and 12 sections, in Korean or English in block letters; many embassies want the Visa Portal e-Form, printed with its barcode |
| Visa fee | The Visa Portal lists USD 40 for a single-entry visa of 90 days or less; fees vary by nationality, so check your embassy’s page. It is not refunded if the visa is refused |
| Result | You check it on the Korea Visa Portal (www.visa.go.kr) |

## Who fills it in

You fill it in if:

- You are a foreign national who needs a visa for Korea and apply at a Korean embassy or consulate: for tourism, business, visiting family, study, training, work or marriage migration.
- You plan to study or work in Korea: K-ETA covers only short visits such as tourism, family visits, events and business meetings, without paid work.
- You are applying for a child under 17: the form is filled in for the child, and a parent or legal guardian signs it.

## Common mistakes

- **Handing in a form your embassy does not accept.** Some embassies accept only the Visa Portal e-Form, printed with its barcode, and refuse handwritten forms; others accept a typed or handwritten form in block letters. Check your embassy’s visa page before you fill it in.
- **Writing a name that differs from the passport.** Question 1.1 asks for your name as shown in your passport. Put the family name and the given names in the right boxes and do not translate or shorten them.
- **Choosing the wrong status of stay.** The code in 2.2 and the purpose in 8.1 must fit the visa you need and the documents you bring. Embassies say it is the applicant’s job to choose the visa type, so check the Visa Portal’s list first.
- **Leaving Yes or No questions unanswered.** The form asks you to complete every question and to tick every box that applies. Answer each Yes or No question, such as 1.8, 1.9, 3.7 and 8.6 to 8.9, and give the details when you tick Yes.
- **Not declaring other names, nationalities or passports.** Questions 1.8, 1.9 and 3.7 ask about them. The form warns that false information leads to revocation of the visa and can lead to criminal punishment and an entry ban.
- **Forgetting the name and signature.** Write your name and sign in section 12. A missing name or signature may lead to refusal. A child under 17 does not sign: a parent or legal guardian does.
- **Applying at the wrong embassy.** You apply at the Korean embassy or consulate for the country or area where you live. Embassies may refuse applications from people who live outside their area.
- **Planning to change a short-term visa in Korea.** The form notes that holders of a category C visa cannot change their status of stay after entering Korea. Apply for the visa that fits your real purpose.
- **Not reporting a new passport.** If you get a new passport after your visa is approved, for example because the old one was lost or damaged, you must tell the visa office so that your details are correct.
