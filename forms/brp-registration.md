# BRP: fields and common mistakes

The first registration (eerste inschrijving) puts you in the Personal Records Database (BRP) of the Dutch municipality where you live, and gives you a citizen service number (BSN). You register in person within 5 days of arriving if you expect to live in the Netherlands for more than 4 months.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Where it goes | The municipality (gemeente) where you live, usually its civil affairs desk (Burgerzaken), by appointment |
| Deadline | Within 5 days of arriving in the Netherlands (art. 2.38 of the Personal Records Database Act, Wet BRP) |
| What to bring | A valid passport or ID card, your residence permit or proof of lawful stay if you need one, proof of address, and original foreign certificates such as your birth certificate |
| Cost | Free of charge |
| Fine | An administrative fine of up to €325 if you do not meet your registration duties |
| What you receive | A BSN, usually sent by letter; several large municipalities say processing takes up to 4 weeks |

## Who fills it in

You register for the first time if:

- You are moving to the Netherlands from abroad and expect to live here for more than 4 months, whatever your nationality.
- You have never been registered in the BRP as a resident before.
- You are a parent or guardian of a child under 16 who moves with you: you register the child, and the child comes along.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for a long-term home before booking.** The deadline is 5 days after you arrive and appointments in large cities can be weeks away. Book as soon as you know your address. If you stay with someone, register there with their consent.
- **Bringing copies instead of originals.** Municipalities ask for original foreign certificates. Bring the originals of your birth and marriage certificates, not photos or copies.
- **Certificates without legalisation or translation.** Depending on the country, a certificate needs an apostille or legalisation, made before you leave. Certificates not in Dutch, English, French or German may need a sworn translation.
- **Names that do not match your passport.** Write every surname and first name as in your passport and birth certificate. Leaving out a middle name or a former surname causes delays and corrections later.
- **Registering at an address where you do not live.** You register where you actually live. Some landlords forbid registration; renting there can block your BSN, and a false address can be fined.
- **Coming alone when your family moves too.** Your partner and children who are registering must come to the appointment as well, each with their own documents.
- **Mixing up your country of birth and your previous country.** Geboorteland is where you were born; In welk land woonde u hiervoor? is where you lived just before coming. They are often different.
