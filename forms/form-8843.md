# Form 8843: fields and common mistakes

Form 8843 is the statement that students, teachers, researchers and trainees on F, J, M or Q visas, and their spouses and children, file with the IRS to explain why their days in the United States do not count toward U.S. tax residence. Each person files their own form for each year they were in the United States, even with no income, either attached to Form 1040-NR or mailed on its own.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The IRS: attached to your Form 1040-NR, or mailed on its own to the IRS in Austin, Texas |
| Who files | Each person who excludes days, including spouses and children, one form each, even with no income |
| Deadline | The due date of Form 1040-NR, including extensions: check the official page for the current year |
| Cost | Free |
| Not a tax return | It reports no income and does not replace Form 1040-NR when you have to file one |

## Who fills it in

You file it if all of these are true:

- You are not a U.S. citizen or green card holder and were in the United States at some time during the year.
- You were there on an F, J, M or Q visa as a student, teacher, researcher or trainee, or as the spouse or child of one (for example F-2 or J-2).
- You are a nonresident for tax purposes because you do not count those days for the substantial presence test.
- This holds even if you had no U.S. income: each family member files their own form.

## Common mistakes

- **Not filing because you had no income.** The form is about your days in the United States, not your income. F and J students and scholars who are nonresidents file it even with no income at all.
- **Forgetting your spouse and children.** Each family member on an F-2 or J-2 visa, including young children, files their own Form 8843.
- **Several forms in one envelope.** Mail each person's form separately, and do not put other forms in the envelope.
- **Filling in the wrong part.** Students complete Part III, teachers, researchers and trainees Part II. Parts IV and V are for professional athletes and medical cases; most students and scholars leave them blank.
- **Visa types for years you were not in the United States.** On lines 7 and 11, write a visa type only for the years you were present, and leave the others blank.
- **Counting days abroad on line 4a.** Line 4a is the actual days you were in the United States. Days you spent outside the country, such as trips home, do not count.
- **Thinking you need an SSN or ITIN first.** If you have no U.S. tax number, leave the box blank. You do not need one to file Form 8843 on its own.
- **Mailing it without a signature or a copy.** When you mail it on its own, sign and date page 2. Keep a copy and proof of mailing: the IRS does not confirm receipt.
