# W-9: fields and common mistakes

Form W-9 is the form you give to a U.S. business or platform that pays you, so it has your correct name and tax number for its year-end tax reports. Only U.S. persons use it, and you give it to the payer, never to the IRS.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The business or platform that asked for it (the requester), not the IRS |
| Who fills it in | U.S. citizens and residents, and U.S. companies; people outside the U.S. use a W-8 form |
| How long it is valid | No expiry date; give a new one when your name or tax number changes |
| Cost | Free |
| Without a tax number | The payer may withhold 24% of your payments (backup withholding) |

## Who fills it in

You fill it in if:

- You are a U.S. citizen, a U.S. resident alien or a U.S. company, partnership, estate or domestic trust.
- A business, platform, bank or broker that pays you or holds your account has asked for it.

## Common mistakes

- **Using it as a foreign person.** A W-9 is for U.S. persons. A person outside the U.S. gives a W-8 (an individual, a W-8BEN) instead.
- **Sending it to the IRS.** The form says it in bold: give it to the requester, do not send it to the IRS.
- **A business name on line 1.** Line 1 holds the name on your tax return. A sole proprietor writes their own name there and the business name on line 2.
- **A name and number that do not match.** A wrong digit or a name that does not match the number leads to backup withholding and notices. Copy the number from your Social Security card or IRS letter.
- **Checking “LLC” for a disregarded LLC.** A single-member LLC that is disregarded checks the box for its owner’s classification, such as “Individual/sole proprietor”, not “LLC”.
- **Filling in an exempt payee code as an individual.** Individuals and sole proprietors are generally not exempt from backup withholding. Leave line 4 blank unless the instructions say you qualify.
- **Leaving it unsigned.** Sign and date the form. Even when the law does not require a signature for some payments, the requester may ask for it.
- **Not updating it after a change.** Give the requester a new form when your name or tax number changes, and when you stop being an exempt payee.
