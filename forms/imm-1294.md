# IMM 1294: fields and common mistakes

IMM 1294, “Application for Study Permit Made Outside of Canada”, is the form from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) that asks who you are, what your passport says, where and what you will study, how you will pay for it, and a set of background questions. Each person applying for a study permit from outside Canada fills in one: most upload it in their IRCC secure account, and only a few people may still send it on paper.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | IRCC. You upload it in your IRCC secure account; paper is only for people with a disability that prevents applying online, or who hold a travel document for refugees, stateless persons or non-national residents |
| Who fills it in | Each person applying for a study permit from outside Canada; a parent or legal guardian signs for a person under 18 |
| How to open it | Download it to a computer and open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader version 10 or higher; it may not open on tablets or phones |
| Edition used here | IMM 1294 (06-2026) E |
| Cost | The form is free; the study permit has a processing fee and most people also pay a biometrics fee. Check the official page for the current amounts |

## Who fills it in

You fill it in if:

- You have a letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution in Canada and are applying for a study permit from outside Canada.
- You apply in your IRCC secure account: the form is on your document checklist.
- You are one of the people IRCC allows to apply on paper.
- You are helping a child: a parent or legal guardian signs the form for a person under 18.

## Common mistakes

- **Typing the DLI number with a zero.** The Designated Learning Institution number begins with the capital letter O, not the digit zero. Copy it exactly from your letter of acceptance.
- **Funds that do not match the proof.** The funds box is in Canadian dollars and should agree with your bank statements, loan or scholarship letters. It should cover at least the tuition and costs you entered in question 4.
- **Gaps in the employment history.** The section covers the past 10 years. If you were in school or not working, say so for that period instead of leaving a gap.
- **Leaving background questions unanswered.** Every question in that section must be answered by applicants 18 or older, or the application is considered incomplete and returned. Check No or Yes for each one.
- **Uploading a printed and scanned copy.** Fill in the form on a computer, click Validate, save it and upload that file. Schools warn not to print and scan it, because the barcode page is added only when the form is validated.
- **Forgetting the PAL or CAQ.** Most applicants need a valid Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter, and students in Quebec need a CAQ. Enter the number and expiry date and upload the letter, or proof that you are exempt.
- **Study details that do not match the letter.** The school name, level, field and dates should match your letter of acceptance. A different start date or program can raise questions.
- **Forgetting the forms that go with it.** Depending on your case you may also need the Family Information form (IMM 5707), IMM 5409 for a common-law partner, IMM 5646 for a minor under 17 without a parent, and IMM 5476 for a representative.
