# Form 80: fields and common mistakes

Form 80, “Personal particulars for assessment including character assessment”, is the Department of Home Affairs form that collects your full personal history so it can check your identity and character for a visa. You fill it in, in English, when the office processing your application asks for it and you are 16 or older.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Who receives it | The Department of Home Affairs, the office processing your application; for an online application you attach the completed, signed form in ImmiAccount |
| Who fills it in | Each applicant aged 16 or over, when the office processing the application asks for it |
| Language | English only: type it in Adobe Acrobat Reader, or print it and write with a pen in BLOCK LETTERS |
| Length | 19 pages, 54 questions in Parts A to T. This guide follows the edition marked “80 (Design date 03/21)” |
| Cost | The form is free to download from Home Affairs; your visa application has its own charge |

## Who fills it in

You fill in Form 80 if all of these apply:

- You are an applicant aged 16 or over: the form says it is completed by applicants who are 16 years of age or over.
- The office processing your application asks for it, in a request from Home Affairs or in your application’s document list in ImmiAccount.
- Each person 16 or over included in the application, such as your partner or an adult child, fills in their own form; never combine two people’s history in one.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving gaps in the timeline.** Questions 17 and 19 must run with no gaps from start to the present. Cover every month, including time between jobs or studies, and explain any gap in Part T.
- **Stopping jobs at 10 years.** The 10-year period is printed for addresses and travel. Question 19 asks for all employment and unemployment and includes the time from your date of birth to your first job.
- **Dates that do not match the rest of your file.** Compare every date with your visa application, CV, skills assessment and passports before you send it. Home Affairs reads them side by side.
- **Missing short or home-country trips.** Question 18 includes visits back to your own country, business trips and short holidays. Work from the stamps in all your passports, old and new.
- **Leaving out a conviction or a charge.** Question 36 asks about pending charges as well as convictions, in any country, and includes a conviction removed from official records. Declare it and attach the court documents.
- **Answering “No” to military service with no reason.** Question 37 asks for reasons even when the answer is “No”. If you did compulsory service, answer “Yes” and give the details.
- **Leaving out family members.** Question 43 includes step-children and children who have died; Question 44 includes step-parents and legal guardians. List them even if they are not migrating with you.
- **Not signing or not dating Part S.** Sign and date the declaration yourself. An unsigned or undated form may be treated as incomplete.
- **Using an old copy of the form.** Download the current PDF from Home Affairs, not a copy saved from an earlier application or another site, and open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader.
